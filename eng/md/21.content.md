## Ecclesiastes 1:1 (id: 10021001001)

1The following is the written lesson of Qoheleth, who was a descendant of David and who ruled as king in Jerusalem.


## Ecclesiastes 1:2 (id: 10021001002)

2Qoheleth said: All of human life is as temporary as the mist of my breath! Everything is emptiness!


## Ecclesiastes 1:3 (id: 10021001003)

3I want to know whether the never\-ending labor of being alive in this world ever produces any sense of satisfaction or joy in this life that makes the labor of life worthwhile.


## Ecclesiastes 1:4 (id: 10021001004)

4One generation of people dies, and another generation is born. Nonetheless, nothing seems to change.


## Ecclesiastes 1:5 (id: 10021001005)

5The sun rises every morning and sets each evening. Then, it hurries back to the eastern horizon, from which it will rise again.


## Ecclesiastes 1:6 (id: 10021001006)

6The wind blows southward. Then, it changes direction and blows northward. It seems to go around and around, and yet, it always seems to go where it is supposed to go.


## Ecclesiastes 1:7 (id: 10021001007)

7All rivers and streams flow into the ocean, but the ocean is never full. Instead, all that water will continue to flow wherever it has always flowed.


## Ecclesiastes 1:8 (id: 10021001008)

8These natural processes make humanity so indescribably tired. Similarly, no matter how much people have seen, they will always want to see more; no matter how much people have heard, they will always want to hear more.


## Ecclesiastes 1:9 (id: 10021001009)

9Whatever happened in the past will happen again. Likewise, whatever people have done in the past, people will do it again. Nothing happens that is ever new.


## Ecclesiastes 1:10 (id: 10021001010)

10Now, someone might claim to have discovered something novel. But, in reality someone discovered it a long time ago, in times long since past.


## Ecclesiastes 1:11 (id: 10021001011)

11No one ever remembers the people or the events of the past. The same will be true of the people and events of the future, and even of those after that!


## Ecclesiastes 1:12 (id: 10021001012)

12I, Qoheleth, ruled as the king of Israel in the city of Jerusalem.


## Ecclesiastes 1:13 (id: 10021001013)

13I resolved within myself to use my intellect and my understanding of the world to study everything that people do during their lives on this earth. I discovered that God has employed every human being in the difficult job of being alive.


## Ecclesiastes 1:14 (id: 10021001014)

14Yes, I observed everything that people do during their lives. Now pay attention! I saw that our lives are as temporary as the mist of my breath. Humanity’s labor is often as silly as trying to catch the wind.


## Ecclesiastes 1:15 (id: 10021001015)

15It is just like people say: “No one can fix what appears broken, and no one can count what is not there.”


## Ecclesiastes 1:16 (id: 10021001016)

16So, I said to myself, “Now pay attention! You know that I, Qoheleth, have become wiser than any other king who has ever reigned in Jerusalem. I have experienced first\-hand what it means to be exceptionally wise and intelligent.”


## Ecclesiastes 1:17 (id: 10021001017)

17Having said this, I resolved to learn more about what it means to be wise, as well as what it means to be stupid and insane, by personal experience. I discovered that even these pursuits were as silly as trying to catch the wind.


## Ecclesiastes 1:18 (id: 10021001018)

18It is like people also say: “The wiser one gets, the angrier and more depressed they feel; knowing more about life only makes life hurt more.”


## Ecclesiastes 2:1 (id: 10021002001)

1Then I said to myself, “Let me experiment. I will try to make myself as happy and satisfied as possible by doing everything that I enjoy.” Now pay attention! I concluded that even the joy and satisfaction I experienced from these pleasures were as temporary as the mist of my breath.


## Ecclesiastes 2:2 (id: 10021002002)

2So I thought deeply and concluded that giddy laughter is the behavior of crazy people. Likewise, I reasoned that merely pursuing what I enjoyed did me no good.


## Ecclesiastes 2:3 (id: 10021002003)

3So I thought deeply about the effect that indulging myself with wine or letting myself behave stupidly might have on me. All the while, I remained sober and alert. I thought about this until I could discern through my experiences the best use of people’s short lives on this earth.


## Ecclesiastes 2:4 (id: 10021002004)

4So I did as many memorable and impressive things as I could. I ordered slaves to build palaces for my leisure and to till and plant vineyards for my pleasure.


## Ecclesiastes 2:5 (id: 10021002005)

5I commissioned royal groves of fruit trees in my honor and gardens with flowing streams. Into those groves, I ordered that slaves should plant many kinds of fruit\-bearing trees.


## Ecclesiastes 2:6 (id: 10021002006)

6Furthermore, I ordered the construction of irrigation pools and canals that provided water for entire forests of lush growth to honor me.


## Ecclesiastes 2:7 (id: 10021002007)

7I purchased male and female slaves, and they bore children who were also my slaves in my palace. I also owned more livestock of herd and flock animals than any other previous king in Jerusalem’s history before me.


## Ecclesiastes 2:8 (id: 10021002008)

8Moreover, I accumulated large amounts of silver and gold that I obtained from the tributary gifts of kings and the rulers of foreign territories. I owned slaves, both men and women, who sang for me, and I married wives and concubines, who provided me with sexual pleasure.


## Ecclesiastes 2:9 (id: 10021002009)

9After all my efforts, I had attained more honor, power, and wealth than any king who had ever reigned before me in Jerusalem. Yet I continued to think soberly so that I could evaluate all of my work properly.


## Ecclesiastes 2:10 (id: 10021002010)

10In all my efforts, I allowed myself to indulge in every desire that I had. Whatever I wanted, I permitted myself to enjoy it. I did this because my difficult projects made part of me happy, and that experience felt like a reward for all my hard work.


## Ecclesiastes 2:11 (id: 10021002011)

11Then I stopped to think about everything I had accomplished, and all the difficult work that I had done to accomplish it. Pay attention! I concluded that both my effort and everything I achieved were as fleeting and insubstantial as the mist of my breath. My labors had been as silly as trying to catch the wind. Nothing I did provided me with any joy that made life worthwhile.


## Ecclesiastes 2:12 (id: 10021002012)

12This led me to start thinking about the potential benefits of being wise alongside those of being crazy or stupid. I did this because, being king, I had more opportunity to investigate these issues than any other human being.


## Ecclesiastes 2:13 (id: 10021002013)

13And then I recognized that being wise is far more helpful in life than being stupid, just like it is easier to live well when one has a source of light by which to see than when there is only darkness.


## Ecclesiastes 2:14 (id: 10021002014)

14It is like people say: A wise person is able to live well, just like someone who walks in the daylight can see. A foolish person, however, endangers themselves with their poor decisions, just like someone who walks about in the dark cannot see. Yet even so, I also recognize that the same outcome awaits both wise people and foolish people: Both will die.


## Ecclesiastes 2:15 (id: 10021002015)

15This made me say to myself, “I am going to die just as inevitably as stupid people! In this way, all my hard work to become a wise person appears to have made no difference! What was the point?” I reminded myself that even my attempts to become wise and live a fulfilled life were as fleeting as the mist of my breath.


## Ecclesiastes 2:16 (id: 10021002016)

16After all, no one remembers anyone who dies, whether they lived wisely or stupidly, for very long. After a while, people will forget about both of them. How sad! No matter whether we live wisely or act like an idiot, everyone will eventually forget about us!


## Ecclesiastes 2:17 (id: 10021002017)

17Because the job of being a human in this life includes enduring these realities that are burdensome, I began to despise being alive. Everything seemed to be as insubstantial as the mist of my breath and as silly as trying to catch the wind.


## Ecclesiastes 2:18 (id: 10021002018)

18Then I began to despise all the difficult work that I had labored to do during my life, especially because after I die, someone else will inherit everything that I have acquired in my life.


## Ecclesiastes 2:19 (id: 10021002019)

19And no one can predict whether my successor will be a wise or a stupid person. Either way, he will have the ability to enjoy all the things that I labored so hard and so wisely for in my life. Regardless, even his enjoyment of the results of my hard work will be as fleeting as the mist of my breath.


## Ecclesiastes 2:20 (id: 10021002020)

20As I thought further about all my life’s work, I began to feel hopeless and depressed.


## Ecclesiastes 2:21 (id: 10021002021)

21Partly I felt this way because it is possible that someone might labor at his work wisely and expertly, even skillfully. But when he dies, his successor will inherit everything, even though he did not do any of the hard work that produced his inheritance! Yet even these painful realities are as fleeting as the mist of my breath.


## Ecclesiastes 2:22 (id: 10021002022)

22After all, the emotionally and physically difficult labor that people do throughout their lives does not seem to produce anything that is both good and enduring for them.


## Ecclesiastes 2:23 (id: 10021002023)

23This is because every day only seems to cause one pain. Furthermore, the work people do only produces grief. It is so grievous that, when they ought to be asleep, people’s minds are so anxious that they cannot rest. Yet even so, all of this is as insubstantial as the mist.


## Ecclesiastes 2:24 (id: 10021002024)

24So, the best way that people can live is to enjoy whatever they have to eat and drink and to allow themselves to find something enjoyable about their work. I have personally experienced that this joy only comes when God gives it to someone.


## Ecclesiastes 2:25 (id: 10021002025)

25After all, there is not a single human being who can enjoy the things he has if God does not first give those things to him.


## Ecclesiastes 2:26 (id: 10021002026)

26God does this by making the people of whom he approves wise, intelligent, and happy. Yet God has decided that whoever disobeys him will work hard only so that they gain wealth that God will give to those of whom he approves. Even their wealth and hard work are as fleeting as the mist of my breath, and as silly as trying to catch the wind.


## Ecclesiastes 3:1 (id: 10021003001)

1There is a fixed moment at which everything in this life happens, and a determined period for every kind of event that happens during a person’s life:


## Ecclesiastes 3:2 (id: 10021003002)

2People will give birth, and people will die, people will plant their crops, and people will reap and harvest those crops.


## Ecclesiastes 3:3 (id: 10021003003)

3People will kill people and people will heal people, people will tear structures down, and people will build structures.


## Ecclesiastes 3:4 (id: 10021003004)

4People will cry and people will laugh, people will grieve and people will celebrate.


## Ecclesiastes 3:5 (id: 10021003005)

5People will throw rocks, and people will gather rocks; people will hug, and people will refuse to hug.


## Ecclesiastes 3:6 (id: 10021003006)

6People will look for lost things, and people will lose found things; people will store things, and people will throw things away.


## Ecclesiastes 3:7 (id: 10021003007)

7People will rip their clothing {in grief}, and people will sew clothing back together; people will stay quiet, and people will talk.


## Ecclesiastes 3:8 (id: 10021003008)

8People will love one another, and people will hate one another; people will go to war, and people will mediate peace.


## Ecclesiastes 3:9 (id: 10021003009)

9Again, I want to know whether the difficult labor of human life ever produces any satisfaction or joy for hard\-working people.


## Ecclesiastes 3:10 (id: 10021003010)

10I have observed the job of living in which God has employed every human being to bustle about doing.


## Ecclesiastes 3:11 (id: 10021003011)

11God makes everything that happens beautifully fitted for the moment that God has determined. And at the same time, God enables humanity to perceive that he is at work in their lives. Even so, God does not allow humanity to understand everything that God does—from the time that he created this world to the point at which he brings all things to an end.


## Ecclesiastes 3:12 (id: 10021003012)

12In light of this, I confess that the best way for humanity to live is to gratefully celebrate life and to live ethically while one is alive.


## Ecclesiastes 3:13 (id: 10021003013)

13And, furthermore, whenever a person is able to enjoy what they have to eat and drink, and to find something gratifying in the work that they do, this is a priceless thing that God has enabled.


## Ecclesiastes 3:14 (id: 10021003014)

14I also confess that all of God’s decisions are unchanging. It is like people say: No one can improve what God does, either by addition or subtraction. God does this so that they submit to God in trusting faith.


## Ecclesiastes 3:15 (id: 10021003015)

15Current events are not new. Things like them have already happened. Likewise, future events will not be new. Things like them are already happening now. God is the one who understands what every person is trying to understand.


## Ecclesiastes 3:16 (id: 10021003016)

16However, here is something else that I observed: The places in society from which people expect rulers to make just and fair decisions are frequently places in which rulers make unjust and oppressive decisions!


## Ecclesiastes 3:17 (id: 10021003017)

17I confessed to myself, “God will hold accountable people who make just decisions and those who make unjust decisions. He will do this because there is a moment for everything that happens, including God’s judgment of rulers in those places.”


## Ecclesiastes 3:18 (id: 10021003018)

18This led me to confess further to myself: “God is governing the world this way because he wants people to clearly understand that they are really no different than animals.”


## Ecclesiastes 3:19 (id: 10021003019)

19After all, human lives and animal lives end in the same way. People die just like animals die, and they seem to possess the same capacity to die. Because of this, it appears that being human offers no benefit over being an animal. All life is as fleeting as the mist of my breath.


## Ecclesiastes 3:20 (id: 10021003020)

20When people and animals die, people bury both in the ground. Both people and animals are as mortal as if God made them from dirt, and when each dies their dead bodies become dirt once again.


## Ecclesiastes 3:21 (id: 10021003021)

21This causes me to wonder whether people are correct about what happens to people when they die. Perhaps humans ascend to the place in which God lives, and animals descend into the ground.


## Ecclesiastes 3:22 (id: 10021003022)

22So, I confess once again that the best way for people to live is to allow themselves to enjoy what their work produces—even the hard work itself. This is because God has given each person their work. It is their daily allotment. After all, nobody can know what will happen tomorrow.


## Ecclesiastes 4:1 (id: 10021004001)

1Next, as I continued to contemplate, I observed all the ways in which people suffer from oppression during their lives. Now pay attention! As I watched those people cry, I realized that no one was there to reassure them in their grief. Furthermore, those who oppressed them appeared to possess power such that no one was able to reassure them in their grief.


## Ecclesiastes 4:2 (id: 10021004002)

2This sad reality made me feel like those people who have already died are much more fortunate than the people who remain alive.


## Ecclesiastes 4:3 (id: 10021004003)

3Yet the people whose mothers have not yet given birth to them are even more fortunate than both people currently alive and those people who have already died. Indeed, the unborn have not yet had to experience or witness any of the evil actions that humans do during their lives.


## Ecclesiastes 4:4 (id: 10021004004)

4Then I observed that people work as hard as they do and strive to be successful only because they are jealous of their friends’ possessions. Yet even their ill\-motivated labor is as insubstantial as the mist of my breath and as futile as trying to catch the wind.


## Ecclesiastes 4:5 (id: 10021004005)

5It is just like people say: Rather than work hard, foolish people merely relax with their unproductive hands folded on their laps, ready to eat their meal. Yet as they do so, they unwittingly ruin themselves, as if they were feasting on their own bodies!


## Ecclesiastes 4:6 (id: 10021004006)

6People also say this: “Despite appearances, being contented with a simple, peaceful lifestyle is twice as good as being rich but living a life of stressful labor—as frustrating as trying to catch the wind.”


## Ecclesiastes 4:7 (id: 10021004007)

7I proceeded to contemplate another facet of people’s lives—one that also seemed as insubstantial as the mist.


## Ecclesiastes 4:8 (id: 10021004008)

8I saw someone who lived alone. He appeared to have no children nor any brothers or sisters living with him. And every day, the person labored without stopping. Even though he was wealthy, he never felt satisfied with his life and possessions. Then he asked himself, “Why am I allowing my hard work to deprive me of things that I enjoy?” His attempts to satisfy himself with a lavish lifestyle were as insubstantial as the mist. His joyless labor was really the most lamentable part of the difficult job of being alive in which God has employed humanity.


## Ecclesiastes 4:9 (id: 10021004009)

9Having a friend is more advantageous than living one’s life alone. After all, together two people can experience better outcomes in their hard work.


## Ecclesiastes 4:10 (id: 10021004010)

10First, if you were to fall down, a friend could help you get up again, but if you are alone and you fall down, you will only experience hardship and pain, because no one will be there to help you stand up again.


## Ecclesiastes 4:11 (id: 10021004011)

11Second, if you and a friend were to lie down to sleep in the cold, you could help one another stay warm, but if you try to sleep in the cold alone, of course you will be cold!


## Ecclesiastes 4:12 (id: 10021004012)

12Third, if someone stronger than you were to attack you, he might beat you up by yourself. However you and your friend would be able to ward off your attacker together! Three people could defend themselves even more easily, just as a rope that someone made from three strands is harder to rip in half than a rope with one or two strands.


## Ecclesiastes 4:13 (id: 10021004013)

13It is preferable to be a poor young man who nonetheless acts wisely than to be a king who, despite his age, acts like a fool and refuses to pay attention to good advice, as he did when he was younger.


## Ecclesiastes 4:14 (id: 10021004014)

14I imagine that it is possible for a young man like that to live so successfully that he becomes king—even ruling over the land in which his mother gave birth to him in poverty, so severe that it was like someone tying her up with ropes.


## Ecclesiastes 4:15 (id: 10021004015)

15But then I imagine all of the people in this young man’s kingdom who merely go about their lives. The person who will be the successor to the young king is among them.


## Ecclesiastes 4:16 (id: 10021004016)

16Despite his current popularity with the endless crowds of his subjects, after a few years future generations will be tired of him. So even the wise way he lived that led to his success is as fleeting as the mist of my breath and as frustrating as trying to catch the wind.


## Ecclesiastes 5:1 (id: 10021005001)

1Be mindful of how you behave when you go to worship God at his house. Draw near to him by listening expectantly. That is far better than offering sacrifices to God in the ways that foolish people do. They are ignorant that they are behaving inappropriately in God’s presence!


## Ecclesiastes 5:2 (id: 10021005002)

2Do not be so quick to say something that you speak rashly. Neither should you allow yourself to raise an issue hastily in God’s presence. You ought to remember that this God is the God who rules from heaven and that you live here on the dust of the earth. For this reason, you ought to think before you say anything in God’s presence.


## Ecclesiastes 5:3 (id: 10021005003)

3After all, it is like people say: “When someone claims to receive secret messages from the gods, he tends to rush busily about his life. And whenever foolish people talk, they tend to speak in as many words as possible.”


## Ecclesiastes 5:4 (id: 10021005004)

4If you solemnly promise God that you will offer a sacrifice to him, you ought to do what you promised quickly. Neither God nor his people tolerate foolish people. You should be careful to do whatever you promise.


## Ecclesiastes 5:5 (id: 10021005005)

5It is better to not promise anything to God than to promise to do something and then fail to do it!


## Ecclesiastes 5:6 (id: 10021005006)

6You should never talk in such a way that you begin to sin. It would be shameful if you promised God that you would offer him a sacrifice only to lie and claim that you made a mistake when God’s priest arrived to collect the sacrificial animal. If you were to do that, God would be so frustrated with your so\-called promises that he would destroy everything you have worked to accomplish!


## Ecclesiastes 5:7 (id: 10021005007)

7This is the point: While everyone else is chasing messages from the gods, offering the prolific prayers of foolish people, or doing whatever vain things people do in God’s presence, you ought to live your life fearfully remembering that God is God!


## Ecclesiastes 5:8 (id: 10021005008)

8You may witness powerful people where you live extorting money from impoverished people or corrupting legal systems to their own benefit. You should not be surprised about this. Lamentably, this is the way things are. The people who can hold these powerful people accountable watch and do nothing. And even these more powerful people are under the authority of someone even higher who likewise does nothing.


## Ecclesiastes 5:9 (id: 10021005009)

9Also, all of these corrupt officials seize the harvests of our fields—even the king enjoys the food that his subjects collect from our farms.


## Ecclesiastes 5:10 (id: 10021005010)

10Some people believe that only wealth can provide them with the kind of happy life that they desire. These people will never think that they have enough money, nor will any number of possessions make them happy. In reality, money is also as insubstantial as the mist of my breath.


## Ecclesiastes 5:11 (id: 10021005011)

11The more wealth that an individual has, the more people seek to benefit from that individual’s wealth. So rich people do not really benefit from their wealth. It only seems to others that their wealth helps them.


## Ecclesiastes 5:12 (id: 10021005012)

12Poor hardworking people sleep soundly at night no matter whether they have a lot of food or only a little to eat. Like a person who sleeps poorly because he has eaten too much, the wealth that rich people believe provides them with a satisfying life actually makes them so anxious that they are unable to fall asleep.


## Ecclesiastes 5:13 (id: 10021005013)

13I have observed that money is the cause of another lamentable, gut\-wrenching situation that can happen to people during their lives. I have watched someone save all of his substantial income. But his wealth ultimately caused him pain.


## Ecclesiastes 5:14 (id: 10021005014)

14When his investments unexpectedly failed, he lost his entire savings. He had planned to give this wealth to his son as an inheritance. Yet now he had nothing left to pass on.


## Ecclesiastes 5:15 (id: 10021005015)

15And so that man died in the same condition as when his mother gave birth to him: He began his life naked, possessing no wealth, and so he died in the same way. When he died, that man’s labor had never produced for him any meaningful sense of satisfaction.


## Ecclesiastes 5:16 (id: 10021005016)

16Yes, this was the lamentable, gut\-wrenching situation that I witnessed: This man brought nothing into the world when his life began, and when he died, he took nothing with him. And so, it seems that his hard work never enabled him to feel satisfied. His work was like a frustrated attempt to grab a handful of the wind.


## Ecclesiastes 5:17 (id: 10021005017)

17Moreover, every day of his life he sat in the dark when he ate his meals—deeply depressed, fatigued from illness, and fuming.


## Ecclesiastes 5:18 (id: 10021005018)

18Now pay attention! I have observed how beautiful life can be when people merely enjoy the food and drink that they have and allow themselves to feel some satisfying happiness in their hard work. People can do this only while they are alive during the brief span of life that God has allotted to them as his gift—no more and no less.


## Ecclesiastes 5:19 (id: 10021005019)

19God sometimes gives people money and possessions. But only sometimes does he also give them the ability to enjoy what they have. When God does, people accept what God has given them—no more and no less—and are able to enjoy their otherwise difficult and stressful work. This joyful acceptance is something priceless, like a gift from God.


## Ecclesiastes 5:20 (id: 10021005020)

20By enabling us to accept the good things in our lives joyfully, God focuses our attention on what we enjoy about our lives. This allows us to forget, momentarily, the reality that one day we will inevitably die.


## Ecclesiastes 6:1 (id: 10021006001)

1Sadly, I have also observed the painful realities that happen to people during their lives. This lamentable part of life is deeply distressing and difficult to accept.


## Ecclesiastes 6:2 (id: 10021006002)

2God sometimes makes people so wealthy and well\-respected that they have everything that they could want. Yet sometimes God does not allow these people to enjoy those things that their wealth provides. Instead, someone outside their family acquires their wealth and enjoys it. Yet even still, money remains as fleeting as the mist of my breath. It is gut\-wrenching and lamentable that this can happen.


## Ecclesiastes 6:3 (id: 10021006003)

3Someone could perhaps father very many children and live a very long life. No matter how long his life might be—even if he were to go on living forever—if he never allows himself to enjoy the wonderful parts of his life, I will consider the life of a baby who dies when it is still in its mother’s womb to be more desirable than this man’s miserable life.


## Ecclesiastes 6:4 (id: 10021006004)

4True, this baby’s life was as brief as the mist, and it died in its mother’s womb, never having seen the light. The baby’s parents were never able to name their child, so that the child’s life will be only a sad memory.


## Ecclesiastes 6:5 (id: 10021006005)

5The stillborn baby did not even live to experience life under the light of the sun. Yet neither did it have to feel the burden of a difficult life. So, despite its brief life, the stillborn baby found more peace than that miserable man.


## Ecclesiastes 6:6 (id: 10021006006)

6His life would not improve even if that man were to go on living for two thousand years. He, like every human being, only ever endured the painful parts of life, but he never enjoyed the good parts of his life. Truthfully, the man’s life will come to an end just as the stillborn baby’s will, and both of them experience death—just like every human being.


## Ecclesiastes 6:7 (id: 10021006007)

7It is like people say: “Everyone works hard to satiate their appetites, but no one ever satisfies their deepest cravings.”


## Ecclesiastes 6:8 (id: 10021006008)

8Consider this: Living wisely does not seem any more able than living foolishly to keep a person from suffering and dying. In the same way, knowing how to conduct oneself wisely does not seem to lengthen or improve the life of someone who is poor.


## Ecclesiastes 6:9 (id: 10021006009)

9It is like people also say: “It is better to enjoy the things that one already has than to allow one’s impulsive desire to inform one’s decisions.” These realities are as insubstantial as the mist, and as futile as trying to catch the wind.


## Ecclesiastes 6:10 (id: 10021006010)

10Before anything happens, God has already declared what it should be. It is evident to everyone that human beings are merely mortal. So, it is pointless to complain to God about the parts of life that we dislike. Humans are not all\-powerful or all\-knowing like God is!


## Ecclesiastes 6:11 (id: 10021006011)

11It sometimes seems as if our complaints about life only produce more words that are as insubstantial as the mist. This does not appear to benefit anyone.


## Ecclesiastes 6:12 (id: 10021006012)

12Certainly, I wonder whether anyone can know what he ought to do during his limited lifetime. People live for a short time, and their lives seem as fleeting as the mist. Indeed, God has designed human life so that it lasts briefly, just like a passing shadow. Moreover, God seems to want human beings to be ignorant about what might happen to them tomorrow.


## Ecclesiastes 7:1 (id: 10021007001)

1An honorable reputation is more advantageous than expensive perfume. Similarly, a person’s death is a more advantageous moment than the moment when that person’s mother gave birth to him.


## Ecclesiastes 7:2 (id: 10021007002)

2Attending a funeral is more useful than attending a celebratory feast. This is true because everyone will die. People should take time to ponder this reality while they are alive.


## Ecclesiastes 7:3 (id: 10021007003)

3Feeling grief and mourning is more advantageous than laughing. This is true because grieving painful experiences may help one better understand what life is like and accept it.


## Ecclesiastes 7:4 (id: 10021007004)

4Wise people participate in public ceremonies of mourning so that they may ponder the fact that they also will die. Conversely, foolish people ignore this reality by only attending celebrations and festive parties.


## Ecclesiastes 7:5 (id: 10021007005)

5Listening to the criticism of someone who lives wisely is more advantageous than listening to the song of foolish people.


## Ecclesiastes 7:6 (id: 10021007006)

6This is true because the laughter of foolish people is as useless as trying to heat food over a fire that someone fuels with thorns. Although the thorns may loudly pop, they will only produce a little heat. In the same way, the loud noise of foolish people is as insubstantial as the mist.


## Ecclesiastes 7:7 (id: 10021007007)

7Now, the power to abuse someone less powerful can turn even wise people into foolish people. In the same way, officials who accept dishonest payment become unable to do what is right.


## Ecclesiastes 7:8 (id: 10021007008)

8It is better for people for something to conclude than for it merely to begin. Similarly, being patient is more advantageous than being proud.


## Ecclesiastes 7:9 (id: 10021007009)

9You should not allow yourself to lose your temper quickly, because being irritable is characteristic of foolish people.


## Ecclesiastes 7:10 (id: 10021007010)

10Neither should you complain and wonder why it seems that life in the past was preferable to the present. Surely only foolish nostalgia, not wise reflection, causes this kind of comment.


## Ecclesiastes 7:11 (id: 10021007011)

11Thinking and living wisely is both valuable and useful, just like receiving an endowment of property or money from a relative who has died. As such, being wise can provide anyone alive with significant benefits.


## Ecclesiastes 7:12 (id: 10021007012)

12Their ability to think and act wisely can provide protection for people, just like money can sometimes provide protection for wealthy people. Nonetheless, knowing how to think and live well has one more benefit than being wealthy: This kind of wise thinking often prevents people from doing foolish things that would cause undesirable outcomes.


## Ecclesiastes 7:13 (id: 10021007013)

13Think carefully about what God has done. Certainly, no human being can change those aspects of their lives that they dislike, since God has determined that their lives should be that way.


## Ecclesiastes 7:14 (id: 10021007014)

14When your life is going well for you, enjoy it! But when your life is not going the way that you would prefer, stop to consider this: God is the one who created life and causes both good things and less desirable things to happen. God does this so that we do not start thinking that we can control what happens to us.


## Ecclesiastes 7:15 (id: 10021007015)

15In my lifetime, as fleeting as the fading mist of my breath, I have witnessed two opposite scenarios. On the one hand, many good people die young, even though they live justly. On the other hand, many bad people live long and prosperous lives, even though they live unjustly.


## Ecclesiastes 7:16 (id: 10021007016)

16So, you should not think that you can be so virtuous that your virtue obligates God to give you a long and prosperous life. Neither should you think that you can become so wise that you can avoid anything painful in life. If you were to think this way, you would only be astonished when undesirable things happen.


## Ecclesiastes 7:17 (id: 10021007017)

17You should not live so sinfully that God becomes angry at you, and neither should you allow yourself to live foolishly. If you were to live this way, your foolishness could cause you to die young.


## Ecclesiastes 7:18 (id: 10021007018)

18Your life will go best if you are diligent in remembering to live according to both parts of my advice to you. The kind of individual who recognizes that God is governing this world and submits to him will demonstrate this by living as both parts of my advice have instructed him.


## Ecclesiastes 7:19 (id: 10021007019)

19The ability to think and live wisely can guide an individual safely through his life. It can do this more effectively than even the ten most powerful officials who govern a city!


## Ecclesiastes 7:20 (id: 10021007020)

20Humanity needs the protection that comes from thinking and living wisely, because no one who has ever lived has been entirely righteous. No human being has ever done only what is good and right without ever doing anything wrong.


## Ecclesiastes 7:21 (id: 10021007021)

21Relatedly, you should not pay attention to everything that people say. If you were to do that, you might inadvertently hear people whom you trust publicly demean you.


## Ecclesiastes 7:22 (id: 10021007022)

22After all, you yourself know very well that you have also publicly demeaned people who trusted you.


## Ecclesiastes 7:23 (id: 10021007023)

23I used my ability to think and live wisely to analyze all of these realities of life. I resolved that I would become wise enough to understand all the realities that I have witnessed in my life. Frustratingly, I could not do it.


## Ecclesiastes 7:24 (id: 10021007024)

24All the present realities that I observed were beyond my comprehension, as if they were too far away for me to see clearly. They were also difficult for me to understand, as if they were too deep for me to get to the bottom of them. I do not believe that any human being can understand all of this.


## Ecclesiastes 7:25 (id: 10021007025)

25Nevertheless, I shifted my attention to a larger task. I wanted to study humanity’s ability to think and live wisely so that I could comprehend the way that God governs everything and the reason that he governs the way he does. I also wanted to study humanity’s tendency to live so foolishly that they openly disobey God and to live so irrationally that they become fools.


## Ecclesiastes 7:26 (id: 10021007026)

26This is what I learned: Foolish thinking is as painful as one’s death. It is like a seductive woman who attempts to ensnare young men. Her desire is to trap and deceive people, as if her very heart were a cage, and her very hands were iron chains. Foolish thinking is like such a woman. Although foolish thinking like that will capture sinful people, those who live well in God’s presence will be able to avoid foolish living.


## Ecclesiastes 7:27 (id: 10021007027)

27So, understand—this is what I, Qoheleth, have learned from my investigations: I carefully considered one thing after another until I could comprehend the way that God governs everything and the reason that he governs the way he does.


## Ecclesiastes 7:28 (id: 10021007028)

28Despite all my failures, I continued searching, but I could not find what I was desperate to discover. I was looking to become wise, as if the ability to think and live wisely was an elusive woman living among a thousand men. I found all the men, but I never found that woman.


## Ecclesiastes 7:29 (id: 10021007029)

29So, consider this: After everything, I have learned only that when God created humanity, God made them to live justly and equitably. Despite this, humans have tried to discover innumerable ways to understand life and attempt to control what happens to them.

2Now, here is my counsel: God supports the king who represents God and rules with his authority. Because of this divine support, you should pay attention to and comply with the king’s directives.


## Ecclesiastes 8:3 (id: 10021008003)

3Do not let this king intimidate you. If the king should ever order you to do something immoral or unjust, you should not comply. Leave immediately! After all, the king has the power to do whatever he wants—including commanding someone to kill you!


## Ecclesiastes 8:4 (id: 10021008004)

4This is because the king possesses absolute authority, such that none of his subjects could disobey him without risking harm to himself. No one would ever dare to question the king’s actions or demand an explanation for what he commands.


## Ecclesiastes 8:5 (id: 10021008005)

5The king will not harm anyone who pays attention to what the king commands and complies with his directives. In light of this, someone who is wise will need to discern how to respond to the king’s authority with careful timing and appropriate behavior.


## Ecclesiastes 8:6 (id: 10021008006)

6They will need to know this because, although human beings endure all sorts of lamentable realities in life, in every situation in which people find themselves, there is always a proper way to do something and an appropriate moment at which to do it.


## Ecclesiastes 8:7 (id: 10021008007)

7One of the most lamentable of these realities is that the events of the future are hidden to human beings. After all, no human can foresee the future well enough to tell others about it.


## Ecclesiastes 8:8 (id: 10021008008)

8God has not enabled anyone to control the wind; in a similar way, no one can control when they will die, just like soldiers cannot decide to go home while they are fighting a battle. This is true even for those people who disobey God. Their attempts to control their own lives apart from God cannot give them mastery over what is uncontrollable.


## Ecclesiastes 8:9 (id: 10021008009)

9As I considered the kinds of things that happen to people during their lives, I witnessed all these lamentable realities happening. I saw that sometimes people like the king possess such power over other human beings that they dominate and harm them.


## Ecclesiastes 8:10 (id: 10021008010)

10Moreover, I observed the honor that people gave to wicked people at their funerals. I had daily witnessed these wicked people going in and out of the sacred places where people worship God, and people in the city praised them for their behavior! Yet even this is only temporary, as fleeting as a mist.


## Ecclesiastes 8:11 (id: 10021008011)

11First, people in power sometimes administer justice against evil actions too slowly. For this reason, many people feel encouraged to plan and commit evil actions without fear of negative consequences.


## Ecclesiastes 8:12 (id: 10021008012)

12Second, sometimes sinful people commit a hundred crimes, and yet they live a long life. Despite this, I am confident that the lives of people who understand that God is powerful and just and good—those who fearfully submit themselves to God—will turn out well in the end.


## Ecclesiastes 8:13 (id: 10021008013)

13I am also confident of the opposite: The lives of people who disobey God will not go well. Just like shadows in the setting sun appear to stretch on forever—in the same way, the current prosperity of evil people is an illusion. ## Ecclesiastes 8:14 (id: 10021008014)

14It is only a temporary reality of this life that the undesirable things that bad people deserve sometimes happen to good people, and the kind of lives that good people deserve seem to happen to bad people. I confess that, despite current appearances, these realities are also as temporary as a mist.


## Ecclesiastes 8:15 (id: 10021008015)

15Living life joyfully is worth celebrating! The best that anyone can do during one’s life is simply to eat and drink and be happy. For as long as this God graciously decides a person should live, this kind of simple joy will remain with him like a faithful companion, no matter what difficult labor he must do.


## Ecclesiastes 8:16 (id: 10021008016)

16I then resolved within myself to become as wise as I could. I wanted to observe the difficult parts of people’s lives so that I could understand them, even though they can make people so anxious that they cannot sleep at night or during the day.


## Ecclesiastes 8:17 (id: 10021008017)

17When I did this, I thought about everything that God has done. Then I realized that he has made humanity so that they can never fully comprehend everything that God does in their lives. Although people will never stop trying to understand, they will never succeed. Even if wise people claim that they understand it all, they really cannot.


## Ecclesiastes 9:1 (id: 10021009001)

1Truly, I thought deeply about all these realities so that I could study them, including this one: It is God who controls what happens to everyone and what the labor that they do produces. This is true for wise people and those who live justly. However, people never know what will happen to them in the future. Even for righteous people, both undesirable and enjoyable things may happen to them.


## Ecclesiastes 9:2 (id: 10021009002)

2Everyone dies eventually, no matter who they are— whether they lived righteously or wickedly, whether they lived well and were ceremonially clean and able to worship God, or they were ceremonially unclean and unable to worship God, whether they offered sacrifices to God or refrained from offering sacrifices. Good people and sinful people will both die, whether they did what they promised God that they would do, or they were afraid to make such promises.


## Ecclesiastes 9:3 (id: 10021009003)

3Yes, this is a lamentable part of being alive on this earth. Eventually everyone dies. But even more so, during the time that they are alive, it seems that everyone is fully intent on living like evil people. They also live like crazy people! And then after that, they die just like everyone else!


## Ecclesiastes 9:4 (id: 10021009004)

4Yet as long as someone is alive, one will have the opportunity to enjoy good things. Because of this, it is better to be alive than to be dead. Even though people think little of dogs in comparison to lions, the dog that is alive is better off than the lion that is dead.


## Ecclesiastes 9:5 (id: 10021009005)

5This is because people who are still alive are aware that they will die someday, but dead people know nothing! Also, dead people have lost their opportunity to enjoy the good things that people can experience during their lives. Furthermore, people will forget about them!


## Ecclesiastes 9:6 (id: 10021009006)

6While they are alive, people love other people. They hate people, and they envy people. However, the things that these people felt died long ago. Those who have died will never again have the chance to enjoy anything good that happens during people’s lives.


## Ecclesiastes 9:7 (id: 10021009007)

7So, go and live your life! Allow yourself to be happy when you eat your food and to enjoy the experience of drinking wine. Remember: God wants you to do this!


## Ecclesiastes 9:8 (id: 10021009008)

8Wear your best clothing all the time, and keep yourself groomed as if you were going to a party!


## Ecclesiastes 9:9 (id: 10021009009)

9Enjoy the experience of living your fleeting life with your beloved wife. God has given you whatever time you continue to breathe, though it is as brief as the mist of my breath. God has apportioned this time for you to enjoy with your wife, even while you labor at your work.


## Ecclesiastes 9:10 (id: 10021009010)

10Whatever you can do, do it with all your energy. You should do this because you will die eventually. When you die and your family buries you in the ground, you will no longer be able to accomplish anything or understand why life happens the way it does—or know or understand anything!


## Ecclesiastes 9:11 (id: 10021009011)

11Once again, I witnessed this reality about life: The person who runs fastest does not always win the race, nor do the strongest soldiers always win the battle, nor do the wisest people always have enough food to eat, nor do the most insightful people always become financially prosperous, nor are the people who know the most always the most successful. No human being can control or predict what will happen to them.


## Ecclesiastes 9:12 (id: 10021009012)

12Even more so, no one can predict when he will die. People catch fish in deadly nets, and people catch birds in traps. Similarly, every human being will experience their inevitable death just like those fish and birds, like a deadly trap that seizes them unexpectedly.

17People will listen to a wise person who speaks softly more attentively than they will to a king who shouts at a crowd of foolish people.


## Ecclesiastes 9:18 (id: 10021009018)

18Being wise is actually a more effective instrument than the weapons that soldiers use to fight wars. Even so, the actions of a single sinful person have the potential to ruin the good things that many other people have accomplished.
